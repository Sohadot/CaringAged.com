Evidence & editorial integrity

Useful guidance begins with clear boundaries.

Later-life care decisions are personal, practical and dependent on location. This page documents the evidence behind CaringAged's introductory framework, what it does not establish, and how sources should be interpreted.

Purpose and scope

CaringAged is an independent editorial concept: a starting point for understanding broad types of later-life support and preparing questions for professionals or providers. It is not designed to assess eligibility, predict health outcomes, rank individual facilities or select care for anyone.

The homepage organises care into four non-exclusive areas: aging in place, support at home, residential options, and respite or transition support. These are practical educational categories; national care systems use their own definitions, funding schemes and regulatory language. Some options overlap or combine.

The visual diagram places the person at the centre rather than presenting care as a one-way progression. People may receive multiple supports at once, move between arrangements, or remain in their chosen home with appropriate services.

Primary evidence and context

Global demography · World Health OrganizationAgeing and health — WHO, 1 October 2025 ↗

Supports the demographic statement that the global population aged 60 and over is projected to reach 2.1 billion by 2050. A projection, not a forecast of customers or the market value of a domain.

United States · National Institute on AgingAging in Place: Growing Older at Home — NIA ↗

Explains planning for continued living at home, home support services, safety, decision-making and the importance of reviewing needs.

Australia · My Aged CareTypes of care — Australian Government ↗

Describes the distinctions between help at home, respite, short-term support and aged care homes. It describes the Australian system, not a universal legal taxonomy.

United Kingdom · NHSGetting a care needs assessment — NHS ↗

Illustrates how assessment and local public services can be involved in identifying daily support needs in England. The process and entitlements depend on local rules.

United States · MedicareLong-term care coverage — Medicare ↗

Clarifies that Medicare generally does not pay for long-term custodial care. This is specific to the US and must not be generalised to other insurance or national systems.

United States · CDCSTEADI: Older Adult Fall Prevention — CDC ↗

A clinical fall-prevention resource intended primarily for professionals; included as context for the importance of safety and assessment, not as a consumer screening tool.

Care is local—even when ageing is global.

The terminology, provider qualifications, level of assistance and public support vary between countries, states and localities. “Assisted living,” “residential aged care,” “nursing home” and “home health” do not always refer to equivalent services or legal categories.

Eligibility and payment depend on local law, the individual's circumstances, coverage and provider arrangements. Cost comparisons require a named jurisdiction, a defined service package and current, verifiable rates. CaringAged does not publish universal price estimates or claim that one option is cheaper or safer for everyone.

Our editorial method

  • Prefer primary sources. Use health agencies, public programs and official guidance for factual statements about care types or eligibility.
  • Separate fact from interpretation. Demographic figures, published service descriptions and the CaringAged conceptual framework are different kinds of claims.
  • Keep geography explicit. Country-specific policies are labelled and are not silently applied elsewhere.
  • Avoid false certainty. No invented accreditation, testimonials, care facilities, provider rankings, partnerships or pricing.
  • Protect agency. The older person's preferences and consent matter; neither a web tool nor a default pathway should replace an appropriate professional assessment.
  • Correct errors. Sources and formulations should be revisited when policies change, or when a credible correction is identified. No automatic live-updating is claimed.

What this site does not do

CaringAged does not provide medical, nursing, legal, insurance or financial advice. It does not operate care facilities, supply care staff, assess individuals, or receive personal health details. The interactive “care lens” presents general questions only and does not calculate needs or store responses.

In an emergency, contact local emergency services. For a care decision or eligibility assessment, consult licensed or otherwise qualified local professionals and official services.
